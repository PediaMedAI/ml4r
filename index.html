About

Rare diseases are individually uncommon but collectively a major global health burden, and most of them begin in childhood. Patients still face diagnostic odysseys that average several years, and only a small fraction of conditions have an approved therapy.

From a machine-learning perspective, rare disease is an extreme long-tail problem: thousands of diagnostic classes, very few samples per class, heterogeneous presentations, and high stakes for every prediction. Evidence is spread across genomes, faces, movement and behavior, speech, imaging, microscopy, clinical notes, and time, which makes diagnosis inherently multimodal.

The Workshop on Machine Learning for Rare Disease (ML4R) treats rare disease as a first-class ML problem. It brings together ML researchers, clinicians, geneticists, and patient communities to turn recent advances, from variant effect predictors and facial phenotyping to knowledge-graph diagnosis and agentic LLM systems, into rigorous, trustworthy, and equitable tools, with particular attention to children and their families. Unlike genomics-focused workshops, ML4R gives genotype and phenotype equal weight and focuses on the clinical path from first symptoms to diagnosis, treatment, and long-term care.

Call for Papers

We invite submissions on machine learning for the diagnosis, understanding, treatment, and care of rare diseases. New models, datasets, benchmarks, and applications are all welcome. Topics include, but are not limited to:

  • Phenotype-driven, genomic, and multimodal diagnosis, including pediatric rare diseases
  • Behavioral and motor phenotyping from video, pose, speech, eye-tracking, and wearable sensors
  • Medical imaging, microscopy, and facial analysis for rare diseases
  • Few-shot, zero-shot, and long-tail learning; synthetic data and patient simulation
  • LLMs, agents, and knowledge graphs for rare disease reasoning and clinical decision support
  • Genomic, protein, and biological foundation models for variant interpretation and gene discovery
  • Drug repurposing, therapy discovery, and clinical trial design for small populations
  • Disease progression modeling, digital biomarkers, and AI for rehabilitation and long-term care
  • Privacy-preserving and federated learning, data sharing, and fairness for rare disease data
  • Datasets, benchmarks, and evaluation protocols for rare disease ML
  • Ethics, patient engagement, and deployment of AI in low-resource settings

Track 1

Long Papers

6–9 pages, ICLR format

Research papers that apply machine learning to rare disease challenges or develop methods motivated by them. References and appendices do not count toward the limit. Selected papers are presented as orals.

Track 2

Tiny Papers

Up to 2 pages

A simple idea with an evaluation, a modest self-contained result, a re-analysis, a new dataset or case study, or a clinical or patient perspective on an ML problem. Ideal for clinicians, geneticists, and junior researchers. Presented as posters; the best receive spotlight talks. Must be primarily human-authored.

Non-archival. We welcome novel, unpublished work. Work already published or accepted at an archival venue (including the ICLR 2027 main conference) is not eligible. Accepted papers are posted publicly on OpenReview and on this website.

Review. Double-blind, at least three reviewers per submission, conflicts managed on OpenReview.
